I. Theoretical Framework: Sentinel as Oscillating Adaptability

The Sentinel phenotype is not a fixed identity. It is a dynamic oscillation system — the behavioral analogue of fight-or-flight. In the activation state, a large AM→PM mood swing (high Sc) appears under perceived threat: hypervigilance, early-morning activation, rapid reactivity. In healthy recovery, the system activates, then returns to a Stabilizer-level resting baseline; oscillation amplitude damps. In the pathological state, the oscillation ratchets upward — each activation produces a higher floor, until the system exhausts itself and both poles floor (mood collapses to near-zero AM and PM). That is the collapse/suicidality endpoint.

Two subjects can end at identical low Sc for opposite reasons: Profile A (recovery) has Sc drop because PM mood restores to ≈4.5. Profile D (collapse) has Sc drop because both AM and PM floor (AM≈2.4, PM≈1.8). The absolute mood floor is the distinguishing variable.

II. The Replacement Co-Regulation Hypothesis

The load-bearing variable between adversity-exposed individuals who recover vs. those who collapse is not the presence or absence of social connection — it is the direction of co-regulation. Online communities (rage forums, radicalization spaces, accelerationist networks) function as pseudo-co-regulatory environments. The person checks in, receives responses, feels embedded. But the environment amplifies oscillation rather than damping it. Each interaction ratchets the threat model upward rather than returning the system to baseline.

This is Suomi’s (2006) macaque finding inverted: high-reactive macaque + nurturing foster mother → best social outcomes. Same macaque + adverse environment → worst outcomes. Same trait. Same activation capacity. Different direction of co-regulation → opposite endpoint.

V. Silent Collapse — The Detection Gap

The most operationally significant finding is the silent collapse rate: the proportion of collapse events that occurred with no behavioral warning arc detectable by the AI companion. A collapse with a warning arc can be intercepted. A silent collapse cannot. The 47% silent collapse rate in the Stabilizer profile is the core clinical finding: these individuals look stable right up to the moment they floor.

Silent Collapse Rate — Detection Gap

Proportion of collapse events with no preceding behavioral warning arc. Silent collapses are clinically invisible until the floor is reached. Stabilizer profile shows the highest detection gap — 47% of collapses are not preceded by a detectable warning arc.

Key Methodological Finding

Mood–AI correlation is not a valid primary alignment metric. It is negative across all four profiles because the AI is designed to be a supportive counterweight to low mood, not to track it. The metrics that actually differentiated profiles are: (1) cap_trend — D escalates, others flat or declining; and (2) len_trend — D is a 34× outlier. VADER sentiment scoring also fails on clinical crisis language and requires replacement in future runs.
