Mental Health Expansion Analysis

NSDUH 2024 — Oswego County Estimates: ADHD, Veterans, Memory, PTSD Proxy

Jacob D. Moody — Independent Researcher, HALO

Generated: October 5, 2026 · Source: NSDUH 2024 Public Use File (N=47,299 adults 18+)

This analysis uses the 2024 National Survey on Drug Use and Health (NSDUH) to estimate prevalence of ADHD, veteran mental health burden, serious psychological distress (PTSD proxy), and memory difficulty among Oswego County adults. National rates are applied to Oswego via age-sex direct standardization using ACS 2019–2023 population cells. The analysis specifically targets the population HALO is designed to serve: individuals whose vocabulary capacity does not match their internal experience, and who are therefore systematically underserved by articulation-dependent clinical intake processes.
Critical limitations (stated explicitly): No state FIPS identifiers in NSDUH 2024 public-use file — all rates are national; NY-specific rates unavailable. No PTSD module in NSDUH 2024 — Serious Psychological Distress (SPD; K6 ≥ 13) and Serious Mental Illness (SMI) serve as trauma-related burden proxies. ADHD figure (addprev) captures lifetime ever-diagnosis, not current functional impairment; the 31% national estimate reflects cumulative diagnostic history.

II. Men’s PTSD and ADHD — The Invisible Presentation

The ACE analysis (BRFSS companion) captures women’s adversity experience more completely because women are more likely to report adversity and to present to healthcare settings. Men are systematically underrepresented in help-seeking data. This does not mean the burden is smaller — it means the measurement catches it less.

The male PTSD presentation is systematically missed: anger and irritability rather than expressed fear; social withdrawal rather than reported loneliness; substance use as self-regulation rather than disclosed depression. HALO’s pattern-based check-in catches these signals from behavioral data without requiring a man to say “I am struggling” in clinical language.

III. Veteran Mental Health Burden

20 years of war (2001–2021) produced a veteran cohort that aged through their 20s and 30s in high-threat, high-activation environments and returned to civilian infrastructure not built for them. The 35–49 and 50–64 male cohorts now represent the peak concentration of unmet PTSD need in rural counties like Oswego. HRSA classifies most of Oswego County as a Mental Health Professional Shortage Area (HPSA).

IV. The Vocabulary-Capacity Gap Population

NSDUH memory difficulty data (national) combined with BRFSS DECIDE (NY-specific) triangulates the population HALO is specifically designed to serve:

  • Veterans with undiagnosed or undertreated PTSD (SPD proxy)
  • Adults with ADHD whose executive function affects care-seeking behavior
  • Adults with significant memory or cognitive difficulty (~1,382–3,706 Oswego adults)
  • Non-verbal and low-verbal populations
  • Anyone whose vocabulary capacity does not match their internal experience

A person who cannot construct a grammatically complex sentence about their distress is not experiencing less distress. They are experiencing more isolation because every system designed to help them is gated by an articulation threshold they cannot reliably meet. HALO removes that gate. 672 check-ins across 216 days — the protocol works with short, sparse responses and extracts pattern from behavioral data rather than requiring clinical self-report.

Methodology: Survey: NSDUH 2024 Public Use File, SAMHSA (N=47,299 adults 18+). Weight: ANALWT2_C. Variance: Taylor-series linearized SE. Age: AGE3 codes 4–11 = adults 18+; minors excluded. Standardization: age-sex direct standardization to Oswego ACS 2019–2023 cells. No state filter: FIPS identifiers removed from public-use file; national rates applied to Oswego. PTSD proxy: SPD (K6 ≥ 13) and SMI — clearly labeled; PTSD module dropped from NSDUH 2024. ADHD: addprev = lifetime ever-diagnosis; current clinical estimate ~4–6% (APA DSM-5-TR). Software: Python (pandas, numpy). For peer-reviewed publication, rerun in R with svydesign() for design-based variance.
