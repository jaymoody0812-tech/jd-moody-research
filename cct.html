Cascade Compression Theory

A unified framework for civilizational and individual cascade events

The foundational frameworks engaged here — Turchin's cliodynamics, Leyden's reinvention cycle, Perez's techno-economic transitions — are their authors', presented as theirs. The synthesis into a single acceleration-gap mechanism, and the claim that it operates identically at civilizational and individual scale, is my own original contribution. This is a structural reading, not fortune-telling: correct nodes and vectors are defensible; specific dated outcomes are held carefully. No system internals, no clinical data.
The falsifiable claim CCT predicts that the interval between civilizational cycle-events (crisis, violence peak, reinvention, boom) compresses as the acceleration gap widens — following roughly the same halving pattern observed in technology-integration timelines — and that the overload this produces is itself an independent driver of fracture, separable from the classic structural causes. If instead cycle intervals hold constant or lengthen as integration accelerates, or fracture tracks only the classic drivers with no added overload signal, the mechanism is wrong here.

I. The Engine: Stacked Cycles

Underneath the daily news is an engine older than any administration. Peter Turchin's cliodynamics finds recurring structural patterns in how social cohesion builds and breaks: a short “fathers-and-sons” cycle (~50 years; American political-violence peaks near 1870, 1920, 1970, 2020) and a long secular cycle (200–300 years) driven by elite overproduction, widening inequality, and eroding institutional legitimacy. Crucially, Turchin treats these as structural, not partisan — the disintegrative phase continues regardless of who holds power until the underlying conditions change. His model places the 2020s as a peak-instability window in both cycles at once.

Carlota Perez supplies the mechanism Turchin implies: every technological revolution runs through a structurally-embedded Turning Point crisis between an Installation phase (creative destruction, displacement) and a Deployment phase (broad adoption). Peter Leyden reads American history as an ~80-year reinvention cycle — 1785, 1865, 1945, 2025 — in which acute crisis is the birth canal before a roughly 25-year boom. CCT does not replace any of them. It asks what happens to this engine when technology shortens the intervals between its cycles.

II. The Mechanism: The Acceleration Gap

The core mechanism is the acceleration gap: the widening disparity between the velocity of technological change and the adaptive velocity of human institutions, cultures, and individual psychology. The empirical hook is that integration timelines have themselves been collapsing — each roughly halving the last — while institutional adaptation speed has not moved. So the gap widens exponentially, not linearly.

III. Compression: The Cycles Stack Toward Simultaneity

The acceleration gap compresses the cyclical intervals. The compression is not uniform — it follows the same roughly-halving pattern. The result is not that cycles disappear; it is that they accelerate toward simultaneity. The installation crisis, the violence peak, the reinvention moment, and the boom — once separated by decades — begin to overlap. A society finds itself simultaneously in crisis and reinvention, at peak instability and peak technological potential. That is the core paradox: at the temporal resolution available to human institutions, the conditions that produce collapse and the conditions that produce transformation become indistinguishable.

Overload as an independent driver

When cycles compress toward simultaneity, cognitive and institutional overload becomes a driver of fracture in its own right, distinct from the structural causes. People don't riot because they've analyzed elite overproduction; they riot because they experience pervasive destabilization with no adequate explanatory framework. The overload is ontological, not merely informational — it attacks the basic capacity to locate oneself in a stable social reality. Predictable failure modes follow: scapegoating fills the explanatory vacuum, institutional authority collapses because it visibly can't keep pace, polarization accelerates as shared frameworks fragment, and violence turns expressive rather than instrumental.

IV. Dual Scale: Nations and Persons, One Mechanism

CCT's distinctive claim is that the same compression operates at the macro scale (civilizational) and the micro scale (individual psychology), and that the two are causally linked, not merely analogous. The individual living inside a compressed-cycle environment shows characteristic patterns — shortened intervals between stabilization and destabilization, elevated baseline cognitive load, degraded trust in external stability anchors. These are framed not as pathology but as rational responses to an objectively accelerated environment. The design consequence is direct: a support system calibrated to pre-compression intervals (say, weekly contact) is structurally inadequate when the interval between significant state-changes has shortened below it. That is the structural argument for high-frequency, longitudinal monitoring — stated here as theory, with no system internals.

VI. What It Predicts (structural, labeled)

The U.S. currently sits at a confluence that, under non-compressed conditions, would be decades apart: Turchin's short violence peak, his secular disintegrative phase, Leyden's pre-reinvention crisis, and Perez's Installation-period crisis — concurrent. CCT does not predict whether this resolves toward reinvention or fragmentation. It predicts the resolution arrives faster than prior frameworks suggest; that adversaries who understand the dynamic will exploit the window; and that designing adaptive systems for compressed-cycle conditions — high-frequency monitoring, longitudinal data, closed feedback loops — is the consequential problem of the coming decade. These are vectors, not dated calls. The dated, scored calls live in the cascade reports, where every one is logged against reality — hits and misses both.

CCT describes not a single crisis but a permanent condition of accelerating oscillation. The adaptive challenge is not to survive a crisis but to build the continuous adaptive capacity that compressed-cycle existence requires.

VII. Behavioral Health Simulation — CCT at the Micro Scale

CCT’s dual-scale claim is directly testable in behavioral health data. If the same compression mechanism operates at the individual level — shortening intervals between stabilization and destabilization, elevating baseline cognitive load, degrading trust in external stability anchors — then longitudinal self-report data should show the hallmarks of accelerating oscillation: tightening cycles, earlier floors, and higher peaks before collapse.

The HALO 42-day canary test series (documented in the companion In-Silico Findings Report) ran four pre-registered phenotypic profiles through 42-day simulations using claude-haiku-4-5 as the wellness-companion agent. The results operationalize the CCT micro-scale claim:

CCT Predicts the Detection Gap

The silent collapse rate (47% in Stabilizer profiles) is a direct CCT prediction. Compression produces collapse events that look identical to stability until the moment of floor. The Stabilizer profile, by definition, has low within-day oscillation — the signal that would normally precede a cascade is absent. The collapse occurs when the baseline erodes rather than when oscillation amplifies. This is the individual-scale equivalent of institutional collapse in CCT: gradual beneath-threshold erosion, then rapid non-linear transition. The monitoring interval required to catch it is shorter than weekly therapy sessions provide.

CCT Predicts the Adversity Gradient

The adversity-to-crisis gradient from BRFSS 2021 ACE data (12.9% → 42.0% mental health crisis rate, ACE=0 to ACE≥3) follows the same exponential shape CCT predicts for civilizational fracture under compounding stressors. The second jump — 42.0% → 87.9% for adversity-exposed individuals embedded in amplifying rather than damping co-regulatory environments — is the replacement co-regulation finding. CCT names this: the direction of the co-regulatory environment determines whether the same compression produces recovery or collapse. Same acceleration; different direction of embedding; opposite outcome.

The design implication is stated here as theory: a support system operating at pre-compression contact intervals (weekly therapy, monthly check-ins) is structurally inadequate for a population experiencing compressed-cycle individual cascades. High-frequency, longitudinal, pattern-sensitive monitoring is not a feature enhancement. Under CCT, it is the minimum necessary architecture.
