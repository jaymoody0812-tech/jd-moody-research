The Sentinel Hypothesis

A Proposed Framework for Adaptive Mood Variability in N=1 Behavioral Health Research

Jacob D. Moody — Independent Researcher, HALO

Draft inception: March 14, 2026 · Revised: June 4, 2026 · Review ready: August 19, 2026

Status: working draft — pre-submission

Standard behavioral health care operates on episodic contact models, typically scheduling weekly therapy sessions that leave 167 of every 168 hours unmonitored. This paper proposes the Sentinel Hypothesis — a theoretical framework suggesting that individual differences in the rate of mood change across time may reflect adaptive phenotypic variation maintained by frequency-dependent selection within social groups. The frameworks engaged here — Wilson’s multilevel selection theory, Clark & Chalmers’s extended mind, Bronfenbrenner’s ecological systems, and Diamond’s environmental determinism — are their authors’. The synthesis and the Sentinel/Stabilizer continuum are original contributions.
The falsifiable claim Individuals exhibiting high within-day mood variability (high Slope of Decay, Sc) represent a functional phenotype maintained by frequency-dependent selection — not a pathology. Pairing a high-Sc Sentinel with a low-Sc Stabilizer produces better co-regulatory outcomes than two matched phenotypes. If longitudinal data shows no fitness advantage to mixed dyads, or if Sc is simply a proxy for disorder severity, the adaptive framing is wrong.

I. The 167-Hour Gap

The dominant model of behavioral health service delivery in the United States relies on episodic clinical contact. A standard weekly therapy session lasts approximately one hour, leaving 167 hours between sessions during which the client’s psychological state goes unmonitored by the treatment provider. For individuals managing chronic conditions such as major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, or ADHD, this structural gap means that the majority of clinically relevant fluctuations in mood, energy, and cognitive functioning occur outside the clinical observation window.

This is not a failure of individual clinicians. It is an architectural limitation of the service delivery model itself. Clinicians rely on client recall at the next session to reconstruct what happened during the intervening week — a process subject to well-documented recall biases including peak-end effects, mood-congruent memory, and temporal compression (Shiffman et al., 2008). The result is that treatment decisions are often informed by a reconstructed summary of the client’s worst or most recent experience rather than a representative sample of their daily functioning.

II. The Missing Evolutionary Framework

Standard psychiatric nosology approaches mood variability through a pathology lens: rapid mood changes indicate dysregulation. But this approach has a blind spot. It does not ask whether some individuals are designed by evolution to be more reactive to environmental change.

David Sloan Wilson’s Evolution for Everyone provides the missing framework. Wilson demonstrates that natural selection does not enforce uniformity. Instead, it maintains phenotypic variation within populations because different phenotypes serve complementary ecological functions. Applied to human behavior: high-reactivity individuals (Sentinels) may function as environmental monitors, detecting threats and opportunities early, while low-reactivity individuals (Stabilizers) provide regulatory stability. Frequency-dependent selection has been documented across animal species — social insects, fish populations, primate groups. The question is whether the same principle applies to human personality and behavioral variation.

III. Theoretical Grounding

Multilevel Selection (Wilson)

Selection operates simultaneously at multiple levels. At the individual level, a Sentinel’s reactivity can trigger rapid response to threats. At the dyadic level, a Sentinel paired with a Stabilizer achieves better outcomes than two Sentinels or two Stabilizers together. At the group level, kin groups with phenotypic diversity outcompete homogeneous groups in variable environments. This multilevel structure explains why complementary relationships form and persist: they are not accidents of chemistry but the expected outcome of selection operating at multiple scales.

Extended Mind Theory (Clark & Chalmers)

Clark and Chalmers (1998) argued that cognitive processes extend into the environment through reliable external resources. A structured self-monitoring system functions as a cognitive prosthetic that externalizes self-regulatory awareness. For individuals with executive dysfunction (ADHD, CPTSD), this externalization is not luxury — it is substitution for compromised internal capacity. A co-regulatory partner (human or AI system) can function as an extended component of a Sentinel individual’s regulatory system, performing the stabilization role that would otherwise require internal effort.

Geographic and Socioeconomic Environment (Diamond)

Diamond’s thesis in Guns, Germs, and Steel (1997) is that geography — not biology, not culture — determined which civilizations developed technology first and at what rate. Applied at the scale of the individual: you do not choose where you are born. Geography shapes your socioeconomic environment. Your socioeconomic environment shapes the frequency, intensity, and chronicity of the stressors your body is asked to respond to across a lifetime. And those activation events shape how your genes express. The candidate genetic variants relevant here — FKBP5, BDNF, COMT Val158Met, OXTR — are not genotype questions. They are genotype-times-environment questions.

IV. The Sentinel–Stabilizer Continuum

Two idealized profiles anchor a continuous dimension. The Sentinel Profile is characterized by high environmental sensitivity, rapid affective responses, frequent mood shifts in response to stressors or opportunities, early detection of environmental change, rapid recovery from negative states, and higher energy expenditure due to frequent state transitions. The Stabilizer Profile is characterized by lower environmental sensitivity, gradual affective responses, relatively consistent mood across contexts, regulatory continuity and buffering for higher-reactivity partners, and reduced responsiveness to acute stressors. These are positions on a continuum, not discrete types.

V. The Slope of Decay (Sc)

The proposed metric quantifying phenotypic position is the Slope of Decay:

Sc = |Mam − Mpm| / ΔT
Where Mam = morning mood rating, Mpm = evening mood rating, ΔT = elapsed hours. Sc = absolute rate of mood change per hour.

High Sc indicates rapid reactivity (Sentinel profile); low Sc indicates stability (Stabilizer profile). By computing Sc across multiple days, an individual’s characteristic position on the continuum can be estimated. Preliminary single-subject estimate: over Phase C qualifying days (n=27), mean Sc ≈ 0.20 mood-points/hour (median 0.15; range 0.00–0.91), consistent with predicted Sentinel-profile variability.

VI. Co-Regulatory Dyadic Model

At the dyadic level, the Sentinel Hypothesis predicts that complementary phenotype pairings produce emergent regulatory properties. A Sentinel + Stabilizer dyad allows the Sentinel to detect environmental changes early while the Stabilizer provides modulation and continuity. Together, they achieve adaptive flexibility neither possesses alone. A Sentinel + Sentinel dyad risks high reactivity amplification and escalation without external modulation.

VII. Gene–Environment Interaction

Genes code for capacity to be reactive or stable. Environment — stress levels, relationship quality, resource availability — gates whether that capacity is expressed. A child carrying genetic markers for high stress reactivity (FKBP5 variants, for example) will display a Sentinel phenotype if raised in an adverse environment but may show moderated reactivity if paired with a regulatory Stabilizer. Suomi’s primate research (2006) supplies the most direct evidence: high-reactive macaques raised in adverse conditions became the most dysregulated animals in the group. The same high-reactive macaques raised by nurturing foster mothers became the most exploratory, socially capable, and high-status members. The trait is not the liability. The environment is the variable.

VIII. Two Collapse Pathways: The Arc and the Silent Floor

The Sentinel oscillation arc produces a visible clinical trajectory: Sc escalates, ratchet_count accumulates, crisis activations fire with the oscillation signal present. Both poles eventually floor. The arc is clinically legible — the oscillation precedes collapse, creating a detectable warning window.

The Stabilizer phenotype produces a categorically different and clinically invisible arc. Sc remains below the activation threshold throughout the entire trajectory. Both mood poles erode gradually across interaction periods — morning and evening scores declining in parallel — until both reach floor. The terminal state is numerically identical to Sentinel collapse, but the pathway left no clinical warning signal. The “calm one” presented as stable until they were already at floor.

Detection gap — canonical finding The same terminal low-Sc state is produced by opposite trajectories. Instruments calibrated on the Sentinel arc systematically miss Stabilizer collapse — not because they are insensitive, but because they are sensitive to the wrong signal. HALO’s AM/PM dual-score architecture is the only deployed instrument that collects the bilateral erosion measure that arc-based instruments cannot produce by design.

IX. Environmental Contingency and Replacement Co-Regulation

Whether Sentinel oscillation is adaptive or pathological depends entirely on the co-regulatory environment. Suomi (2006) demonstrated in rhesus macaque studies that high-reactive individuals placed with nurturing foster mothers became the most socially competent animals in the cohort; the same genotype in adverse environments produced the worst outcomes. The trait carries the capacity. The environment sets the direction.

An individual embedded in a rage forum, radicalization network, or accelerationist community experiences something structurally similar to co-regulation. But those environments amplify oscillation rather than damping it. This is replacement co-regulation: a pseudo-co-regulatory environment that inverts the function of genuine co-regulation. Community adults with ACE=0 show 12.9% mental health crisis rates (BRFSS 2021); community adults with ACE ≥ 3 show 42.0%; adversity-exposed individuals in the Violence Project mass shooters dataset show 87.9%. Isolation plus replacement co-regulation is the proposed mechanism for the 45.9 percentage-point second jump.

Bowlby Convergence

Bowlby’s (1969) attachment theory describes the “secure base” as a responsive co-regulatory presence that enables the attached person to explore, fail, return, repair, and go out again. Without this presence, the attached person either collapses or repeats the error. This is structurally identical to the Sentinel-Stabilizer pairing mechanism. This convergence was not derived from Bowlby’s work — the Sentinel Hypothesis was developed from HALO caregiver data and lived experience of caregiving under structural pressure. Independent replication across 70 years and two methodological traditions strengthens the theoretical claim: the presence or absence of one warm, responsive relational presence is not merely helpful — it is the structural mechanism that determines whether silent collapse occurs.

XI. Candidate Genetic Basis

Months before the Sentinel Hypothesis existed as a named framework, I accessed my consumer-grade direct-to-consumer genomic data and identified variants associated with mood reactivity, stress response magnitude, executive function, and co-regulatory capacity. My data aligns with a Sentinel-profile prediction for: COMT (Val158Met) — dopamine metabolism, differential stress reactivity and executive-function variability. FKBP5 (rs9470080) — glucocorticoid receptor sensitivity, risk alleles consistent with CPTSD diagnosis and ACE score of 9. BDNF (Val66Met) — neuroplasticity, mood variability and vulnerability to stress-related mood episodes. OXTR (rs53576) — oxytocin receptor, affiliative behavior and stress buffering through close relationships. These are hypothesis-generating findings, not clinical conclusions.

XII. Research Agenda

Formal validation of the Sentinel Hypothesis requires: (1) psychometric validation of the Sc metric across a multi-participant sample; (2) cross-participant phenotype identification to test the predicted continuum distribution; (3) dyadic pairing studies to test complementary co-regulation predictions; and (4) longitudinal tracking to test whether Sentinel expression attenuates with sustained exposure to consistently safe environments. The N=1 pilot data presented in the companion case study (“HALO as Extended Mind”) represents a proof-of-concept for the data-collection methodology.
