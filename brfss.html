BRFSS 2021 New York — Oswego County Estimates

Jacob D. Moody — Independent Researcher, HALO

Generated: October 5, 2026 · Source: CDC BRFSS 2021, NY subsample (N=54,810)

This analysis estimates the number of Oswego County adults who experience serious difficulty concentrating, remembering, or making decisions — the DECIDE variable from the 2021 CDC Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS), New York state subsample. This population represents people whose internal cognitive capacity is not visible through standard interaction or conversation: the vocabulary-capacity gap. Note: The full cognitive decline module (CIMEMLOS, CDHOUSE, CDSOCIAL) was present in the NY 2021 BRFSS file but contained no valid responses — NY ran the module header but did not collect data. DECIDE is the valid available measure and is the most clinically relevant variable for HALO’s target population.

II. ACE Score and Mental Health Crisis Rate — Oswego County Context

Adverse Childhood Experience (ACE) scores from the BRFSS 2021 ACE module provide the adversity gradient underlying cognitive impairment risk in the county. The relationship is dose-dependent: higher ACE scores predict higher rates of adult mental health crisis, cognitive difficulty, and executive function impairment.

V. Clinical Relevance — The Vocabulary-Capacity Gap

The DECIDE population is precisely the group whose internal experience exceeds their ability to describe it. Standard clinical intake depends on a person’s ability to articulate distress in structured language. This population cannot reliably do that — not because they aren’t experiencing distress, but because executive function impairment makes that articulation inconsistent or impossible.

Young adults show the highest rates: Female 18–24 at 21.6%; Male 18–24 at 16.3%. These are the ages at which behavioral health crises most commonly emerge and at which clinical engagement is most likely to prevent long-term trajectory entrenchment.

HALO removes the articulation gate. The check-in protocol requires minimal language. Patterns emerge from behavioral data over time without requiring the user to diagnose themselves or explain their state. The tool adapts to the user’s register rather than requiring the user to adapt to the tool.

Methodology: Survey: CDC BRFSS 2021, NY Landline + Cellular sample (N=66,124 raw; 55,203 completed). Weight: \_LLCPWT2. Variance: Taylor-series linearized SE, respondent-level Bernoulli approximation. Exclusions: DISPCODE ≠ 1100; DECIDE = 7/9/blank; missing sex/age/weight. ACS: 2019–2023 5-year estimates, Oswego County NY, Table B01001. Software: Python (pandas, numpy). Limitation: NY statewide rates applied to Oswego; intra-county variation not captured. For peer-reviewed publication, rerun in R with svydesign() for design-based variance.
